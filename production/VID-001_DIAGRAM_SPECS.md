# VID-001 Diagram and Chart Specifications

## D01 — Core Grid-to-Data-Center Diagram

### Purpose
The recurring visual spine of the entire video.

### Elements
- generation icon/group
- high-voltage transmission line
- bulk-power substation
- transformer
- distribution/direct-service branch
- data-center building
- optional battery/onsite generation at site

### Animation states
1. empty site;
2. nearby transmission shown;
3. service path highlighted but incomplete;
4. interconnection-study zone highlighted;
5. upgrades added;
6. capacity/operations/risk overlays;
7. completed service path.

### Accuracy rule
Do not imply every data center is served through the same voltage/configuration. Label as **Simplified illustrative grid path**.

---

## D02 — Connection vs Interconnection

Use nested visual scopes.

**Interconnection**
Narrow highlighted segment where the proposed load is studied/attached to transmission or distribution.

**Connection**
Larger frame encompassing:
- forecast/planning;
- interconnection;
- required construction;
- resource/capacity;
- operations;
- service terms/cost allocation.

Label this as a **VID-001 explanatory simplification derived from Berkeley Lab**, not a reproduction of a Berkeley figure.

## D03 — Global Data-Center Electricity Chart

Source:
IEA, Key Questions on Energy and AI, April 2026.

Data:
- 2025: 485 TWh
- 2030 central projection: ~950 TWh
- AI-focused data-center electricity: roughly triples over same period

Preferred graphic:
two primary bars for all-data-center demand plus a secondary annotation for AI-focused growth.

Required labels:
- Global
- 2025 estimate
- 2030 projection
- All data centers
- AI-focused series called out separately

Do not visually imply that 950 TWh is guaranteed.

## D06 — ATC Bounded Timeline

Use one horizontal timeline with a strong disclaimer.

Planning:
~6–18 months

Selected construction ranges from the reviewed guide:
- shorter transmission-line extension: roughly 18–30 months
- longer extension: roughly 30–48 months
- complex work/long-lead equipment or certain approvals: roughly 48–60 months

Permanent label:
**ATC example — not a U.S. average**

Do not graph these as a probability distribution.

## D07 — Forecast / Stranded-Cost Illustration

This is fictional.

Frame A:
requested capacity rises to planned level.

Frame B:
utility/network build follows expected load.

Frame C:
actual load ramps slowly or stops below expected demand.

Frame D:
infrastructure cost remains.

Label:
**Illustrative — not a real project**

---

## D08 — Battery: Power vs Energy

Two visual dimensions:

**Power (MW):** how fast electricity can be delivered.

**Energy (MWh):** how much can be delivered over time.

Use a faucet/tank or pipe/reservoir analogy only if labels remain visible.

Avoid suggesting a fixed duration for “a data-center battery” because systems vary.

---

## D09 — Three-Question Final Card

1. Where will its electricity come from?
2. What has to be built to get it to the site?
3. What happens when demand changes?

This becomes a reusable channel motif for future infrastructure videos.
