# VID-001 Thumbnail Production Briefs V1

## General constraints

- 16:9 YouTube thumbnail.
- Must remain legible on a phone.
- One core visual idea, not a collage.
- Do not imply a real outage or emergency.
- Avoid logos unless necessary.
- No competitor imitation.
- Data center should read as a large computing facility, not an ordinary office.
- Grid equipment should look recognizably electrical but need not be a specific utility's installation.

## Variant A — WAITING FOR POWER

**Associated title:** Why Data Centers Can't Just Plug Into the Grid

### Composition
Left:
large modern data-center building / server-campus silhouette.

Right:
substation and transmission line.

Center:
obvious incomplete electrical connection or visible gap.

### Text
**WAITING FOR POWER**

Maximum three words.

### Visual question
“If the power infrastructure is right there, why isn't the building connected?”

### Avoid
- broken plug cartoon as the only concept;
- red emergency lights;
- blackouts/city darkness;
- tiny unreadable equipment detail.

**Current lead thumbnail hypothesis.**

---

## Variant B — TWO DIFFERENT SPEEDS

**Associated title:** The Power Grid Wasn't Built for the AI Boom

### Composition
One side:
rapidly multiplying server racks/data-center buildings.

Other side:
large transformer/substation under construction.

Use directional motion to imply fast digital build versus slower physical infrastructure.

### Text
**TWO DIFFERENT SPEEDS**

### Risk
The title can overgeneralize regional grid conditions. Keep only as a packaging test, not the factual lead.

---

## Variant C — THE MISSING LINK

**Associated title:** AI's Power Problem Goes Beyond Power Plants

### Composition
Three-stage visual:
power generation → highlighted missing/strained grid connection → data center.

The center connection is the visual hero.

### Text
**THE MISSING LINK**

### Goal
Test whether the mechanism itself is more intriguing than the “waiting” framing.

---

## Thumbnail evaluation sheet

Score each draft only as an internal design check, not a prediction of CTR:

- Can the idea be understood at small size?
- Does it create one clear question?
- Does it match the title rather than repeat it?
- Is the data center instantly recognizable?
- Is the grid connection visually obvious?
- Is there visual clutter?
- Could any element imply a false real-world incident?
- Would we still understand it with thumbnail text hidden?

Do not call a design “proven” before channel testing.
