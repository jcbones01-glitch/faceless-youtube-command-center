# VID-001 Narration Direction V1

## Performance goal

Sound curious, competent and conversational — like explaining a surprising physical system to an intelligent friend.

Avoid:
- movie-trailer intensity;
- breathless AI-hype delivery;
- cynical “everything is broken” tone;
- fake urgency;
- reading citations aloud.

## Pace

Clean script: 1,438 words.

Planning range:
- 145 wpm ≈ 9.9 minutes
- 160 wpm ≈ 9.0 minutes

Target first sample:
**150–155 wpm average**, with slower pauses around the hook, ATC disclaimer, and final three questions.

Do not force a constant pace. Diagrams need breathing room.

## Emphasis map

### Opening
Pause after:
“Where do you plug it in?”

Give “This project is fictional, but the problem is real” a calm, clarifying delivery.

### Forecasts
Emphasize:
“projections, not destiny.”

Do not sell the forecast as certainty.

### Connection vs interconnection
Slow slightly. This distinction is central to the video's intellectual payoff.

### ATC example
Emphasize:
“one provider's process”
and
“not a national average.”

### Risk section
Shift from engineering to economics naturally. The rhetorical question about who takes the risk should feel explanatory, not accusatory.

### Flexibility
“Sometimes.” should stand alone briefly.
Then deliberately counter the idea that flexibility is magic.

### Ending
Leave a small pause between the three questions.

Transformer tease should be understated, not a hard sell.

## Pronunciation guide

- IEA: “eye-ee-ay”
- TWh in narration: say “terawatt-hours”
- MW: usually say “megawatts”
- Berkeley Lab: “BERK-lee Lab”
- Portland General Electric: full name first; “P-G-E” only afterward if used
- American Transmission Company: full name first; “A-T-C” afterward
- gas-insulated substation: “gas IN-suh-lay-tid sub-STAY-shun”
- interconnection: “in-ter-kuh-NEK-shun”
- procurement: “pro-KYOOR-ment”

## Voice-sample test

Before generating/recording the full narration, make a 35–50 second sample containing:
- the opening question;
- one technical sentence;
- one number;
- the phrase “interconnection”;
- one deliberate pause.

Review for:
- naturalness;
- intelligibility on phone speaker;
- number clarity;
- technical pronunciation;
- over-dramatization;
- breath/pause rhythm.

Do not purchase a voice subscription before this short sample demonstrates a real need.
